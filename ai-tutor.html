At a glance

Challenge

Design an AI tutoring product from zero for two audiences, then realign a fast-moving live build.

Approach

Brand first, then both portals. An audit of the live build kept what worked.

Outcome

Figma became the single source of truth: one AI identity and three navigation items.

Final design

The result

The problem

Where it started

  1. 01

    Two names, four tabs, buried tasks

    Another brand name, a generic tutor and settings weighted like daily tasks in a four-item sidebar.

  2. 02

    One number, two meanings

    Long-term progress and test accuracy looked like one metric, so parents could not tell them apart.

  3. 03

    Nothing between upload and answer

    The audit rated missing loading and error states as P0 blockers. Accessibility scored 4.5 out of 10.

Process

How it ran

  1. 01 Discover Brief and brand strategy, then the identity and app icon.
  2. 02 Audit 22 findings scored P0 to P4, then the live product.
  3. 03 Structure 3 navigation items, settings in the user menu.
  4. 04 Design & system Component library, onboarding, gamification and one AI identity.
  5. 05 Ship A dated decision log and module specs.

Key decisions

Three calls that shaped it

01

Three items in the top navigation

Parents come for a family overview, help with homework and the curriculum. Settings moved to the user menu, and child switching to an avatar picker.

Details
Rejected
The four-item sidebar, giving settings the weight of daily tasks.
Trade-off
Settings sit one click deeper, fine for something rarely used.
02

Mastery and score are different numbers

Progress and accuracy were mixed. Mastery now leads on the family dashboard and the score lives in the full report, each with a tooltip.

Details
Rejected
One blended percentage, simpler but misleading for parents.
Trade-off
Two metrics to explain, handled with short tooltips.
03

Make the AI wait visible

Analysis takes several seconds, so a dedicated Analyse screen with visible steps now sits between upload and result.

Details
Rejected
Dropping the loading screen, an earlier call reversed after the audit.
Trade-off
One more screen, and in exchange parents trust what is happening.

Outcome

What the team got

  • 42 Issues from two prototype audits, each rated P0 to P4
  • 4→3 Top-level navigation items after the IA rework
  • 5 Visible steps between a homework photo and its explanation
