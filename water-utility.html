Water utility web

A task-first web presence for two water operators, proposed so paying, reporting and checking outages take seconds.

  • Pitch concept
  • Information architecture
  • Accessibility
  • Benchmark

2 min read

At a glance

Challenge

Two sites on different stacks, an institutional menu, and key tasks buried under “Useful information”.

Approach

An audit of both sites and a European benchmark led to 5 task pillars and a design system.

Outcome

A build-ready proposal: 104 traced requirements, a task-first sitemap and a design system draft.

The problem

Where it started

  1. 01

    Tasks buried under information

    Billing sat three levels deep, payment needed an account and outages went through Contact.

  2. 02

    Every action, everywhere

    An 8-slide carousel led the homepage, and outages were a text list with no map.

  3. 03

    A fragile, non-compliant base

    15 of 19 images had no alt text, zoom was blocked and menu contrast was 2.5:1.

Key decisions

Three calls that shaped it

01

Five task-first pillars

Customers come to pay, report a problem or check an outage. The sitemap puts those tasks first: customers, services, water quality, about us and the online account.

Details
Rejected
Keeping the institutional menu with a new look.
Trade-off
Editorial content moves one level down.
02

One intent in the hero

An 8-slide carousel and about ten targets left no clear first step. The hero now asks one thing, and every task lives in one quick-actions band.

Details
Rejected
Big buttons for every task in the hero.
Trade-off
Payment sits one scroll lower, but always in the same place.
03

Reliability is part of the design

Comparable utility apps score 2.4 and 1.3 stars after visual redesigns on unstable backends. The proposal treats errors, payment and login reliability as design work.

Details
Rejected
A visual refresh over the same backend.
Trade-off
A larger scope for the first phase.

Outcome

What the team got

  • 104 Requirements traced from the brief into the proposal
  • 17 Utility operators benchmarked, at home and abroad
  • 12→5 Department menu items regrouped into task pillars
